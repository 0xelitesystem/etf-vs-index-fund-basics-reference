# How they trade

The most visible difference is timing: an ETF trades throughout the day at changing market prices, while an index mutual fund transacts once a day at a single closing price. This affects how and when you can buy or sell, the kinds of orders you can use, and how precisely you know the price you will get.

## ETFs trade intraday

An ETF trades on an exchange during market hours, so its price moves moment to moment with supply, demand, and the value of its holdings. You can buy or sell at any time the market is open, see a live price, and use order types familiar from stock trading, such as limit orders that set a maximum buy or minimum sell price. This flexibility suits investors who want intraday control, though for long-term holders it rarely matters which minute they trade.

## Mutual funds price once daily

An index mutual fund does not trade intraday. Orders placed during the day all execute at the same net asset value, calculated once after the market closes based on the fund's holdings. You do not know the exact price at the moment you place the order; you get that evening's value. This is simpler in some ways, with no live price to watch and no spread to cross, but it removes the ability to react to prices within the day.

## What the difference means in practice

For a long-term investor making periodic contributions, once-a-day pricing is perfectly adequate and arguably calmer, since there is no temptation to time intraday moves. For someone who wants to trade precisely, set limit prices, or move quickly, the ETF's intraday trading is an advantage. Neither approach is inherently better; they suit different habits. The trading mechanism is a difference in convenience and control, not in the underlying investment, which can be identical.
