# ETF vs Index Fund Basics

A plain-language reference comparing exchange-traded funds (ETFs) with index mutual funds. Both can track the same index and hold the same stocks, yet they differ in how they are structured, how they trade, what they cost, how they are taxed, and what minimums they require. This reference explains those differences and when each tends to make more sense.

## The core idea

An ETF and an index mutual fund can hold the same basket of investments, but an ETF trades on an exchange like a stock throughout the day while an index mutual fund is bought and sold once daily at its closing net asset value. The practical differences flow from that structure: ETFs trade intraday and often have no minimum beyond one share's price, while mutual funds price once a day and may require a minimum investment. ETFs are usually more tax-efficient in taxable accounts because of how shares are created and redeemed, while expense ratios on broad index products are low and competitive in both forms.
